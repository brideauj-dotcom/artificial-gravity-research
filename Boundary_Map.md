# Boundary Map

This file records constraints, no-go results, and hard limits. Boundaries can be revisited, but only by naming the assumption being challenged.

## Known Boundaries To Map

### B-001: Equivalence Principle

Artificial gravity can be locally mimicked by acceleration, but global gravitational fields and tidal structures are not identical in every context.

### B-002: Stress-Energy Requirements

In general relativity, spacetime curvature is sourced by stress-energy. Engineered gravity-like fields require mass-energy distributions or equivalent stress-energy configurations that may be impractically large.

### B-003: Energy Conditions

Many spacetime-engineering ideas require violations of classical energy conditions. Quantum field theory allows limited negative energy effects, but known constraints may sharply limit macroscopic use.

### B-004: Human Factors In Rotating Habitats

- **Claim:** Rotational artificial gravity is physically straightforward but constrained by radius, RPM, Coriolis effects, vestibular adaptation, gravity gradients, structure, and mass.
- **Why it matters:** This is the strongest established artificial-gravity path, so its numerical limits define the baseline that speculative mechanisms must beat.
- **Strength:** established for the mechanics; human comfort and minimum-dose thresholds remain incompletely mapped.
- **Useful scale:** Using `a = omega^2 r`, the required radii are:

| Target | 2 rpm | 4 rpm | 6 rpm | 10 rpm |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `0.3g` | `67.1 m` | `16.8 m` | `7.5 m` | `2.7 m` |
| `0.5g` | `111.8 m` | `27.9 m` | `12.4 m` | `4.5 m` |
| `1.0g` | `223.6 m` | `55.9 m` | `24.8 m` | `8.9 m` |

- **Coriolis note:** For `1 m/s` radial walking, `a_C = 2 omega v`, giving about `0.04g` at `2 rpm`, `0.09g` at `4 rpm`, `0.13g` at `6 rpm`, and `0.21g` at `10 rpm`. This term depends on spin rate and motion, not on radius directly.
- **Gravity-gradient note:** Over a `1.8 m` standing body, fractional head-to-foot variation is approximately `1.8/r`. At `1g`, that is `0.8%` for `2 rpm`, `3.2%` for `4 rpm`, `7.2%` for `6 rpm`, and `20%` for `10 rpm`.
- **Assumptions:** Continuous rotating habitat; ordinary human movement; no active vestibular mitigation beyond training/adaptation; rigid-body kinematics.
- **Possible cracks:** Intermittent artificial gravity may require much less habitable rotating volume; progressive adaptation may allow higher RPM; exercise-coupled centrifuges may produce health benefits without comfort during free locomotion.
- **Sources:** Clément, Bukley, and Paloski 2015 review; standard rotating-frame mechanics.

### B-005: FTL Causality Problems

Faster-than-light travel generally creates causality issues in relativity. Any proposal must address chronology protection, horizons, stability, and global spacetime structure.

### B-006: Ordinary-Matter Gravitomagnetic Magnitude

- **Claim:** Frame dragging from rotating ordinary matter is real but far too small to create useful artificial gravity at laboratory scales.
- **Why it matters:** This blocks a common "spinning mass makes artificial gravity" intuition. In weak-field GR, the relevant scale is set by angular momentum and suppressed by `G/c^2`; high RPM alone is not enough.
- **Strength:** established for standard GR weak-field physics; engineering conclusion is robust by order-of-magnitude.
- **Assumptions:** Uses weak-field, slow-motion general relativity; ordinary positive mass-energy; no anomalous material coupling; no exotic stress-energy.
- **Possible cracks:** Non-ordinary stress-energy distributions, compact astrophysical-density matter, or replicated non-GR anomalies. These would be new physics or inaccessible engineering regimes, not ordinary frame dragging.
- **Sources:** Ruggiero & Tartaglia 2002; Everitt et al. 2011 Gravity Probe B; Ruggiero & Astesiano 2023.

### B-007: Localized Field Generation Requires Local Stress-Energy Or New Coupling

- **Claim:** Under standard general relativity, a localized real gravitational field requires a corresponding stress-energy distribution; ordinary laboratory energy densities source extremely small curvature.
- **Why it matters:** This is the central boundary for the user's preferred direction: localized field generation must beat the stress-energy scaling problem, identify a non-ordinary source term, or move into experimentally testable beyond-GR physics.
- **Strength:** established for standard GR; speculative only where modified gravity, exotic stress-energy, or unreplicated anomalies are invoked.
- **Assumptions:** Einstein field equations hold in the laboratory regime; equivalence principle and precision gravity tests remain valid; ordinary positive mass-energy and electromagnetic fields gravitate according to their standard stress-energy tensors.
- **Possible cracks:** High energy-density fields, negative-energy configurations constrained by quantum inequalities, strong-field analogies that suggest new metric-engineering mathematics, or replicated deviations from GR/equivalence-principle expectations.
- **Sources:** Developed through E-009 and B-008 through B-020: standard GR stress-energy accounting, energy-condition/QEI literature, precision gravity constraints, and explicit modified-coupling benchmarks.

### B-008: Ordinary Electromagnetic Field Energy Is Gravitationally Tiny

- **Claim:** Electromagnetic fields do source gravity in standard GR, but accessible field energies are far too small to produce useful localized artificial gravity.
- **Why it matters:** This blocks the tempting localized-field shortcut: "make a very strong electric or magnetic field and let its stress-energy act as gravity." The source term is real, but the required energy density is astrophysical.
- **Strength:** established for standard EM stress-energy plus order-of-magnitude Newtonian/weak-field gravity; detailed relativistic stress/tension terms add nuance but not rescue at laboratory scales.
- **Useful scale:** `u_B = B^2/(2 mu0)` and `rho = u_B/c^2`. A `45.5 T` continuous magnet gives `u ~= 8.2e8 J/m^3` and `rho ~= 9.2e-9 kg/m^3`. A `1200 T` destructive/pulsed scale gives `u ~= 5.7e11 J/m^3` and `rho ~= 6.4e-6 kg/m^3`. A `1 m` radius sphere producing `1g` at its surface needs `M ~= 1.5e11 kg`, `E ~= 1.3e28 J`, and average `u ~= 3.2e27 J/m^3`, equivalent to `B ~= 8.9e10 T`.
- **Assumptions:** Standard Einstein-Maxwell coupling; weak-field order-of-magnitude estimate; no exotic media or anomalous coupling; human-scale field region; ordinary positive EM energy.
- **Possible cracks:** Nonlinear electrodynamics at extreme fields, negative-energy configurations, modified gravity, or compact astrophysical regimes. These are not near-term EM magnet engineering and require separate source-specific constraints.
- **Sources:** Navarro & Sancho 2011; Gibbons & Herdeiro 2001; Tsagas & Mavrogiannis 2021; Hahn et al. 2019; standard EM energy density formula.

### B-009: Casimir Negative Energy Is Real But Not Macroscopic Gravity Engineering

- **Claim:** Casimir and other QFT negative-energy effects violate classical pointwise energy conditions, but known configurations do not provide useful localized artificial gravity.
- **Why it matters:** This is the most tempting "localized field generation" crack after ordinary EM stress-energy fails: if negative energy could be engineered freely, it would affect wormhole, warp, and repulsive-gravity thinking. The actual constraints are much tighter.
- **Strength:** established that Casimir/QFT negative energy exists; likely/strongly modeled that quantum energy inequalities and integrated apparatus stresses block macroscopic engineering uses under known physics.
- **Useful scale:** For ideal parallel plates, `|u| = pi^2 hbar c/(720 a^4)`. At `a = 1 um`, `|u| ~= 4.3e-4 J/m^3`; at `a = 10 nm`, `|u| ~= 4.3e4 J/m^3`; at `a = 1 nm`, `|u| ~= 4.3e8 J/m^3`. The crude `1 m` spherical `1g` benchmark from B-008 needs average `u ~= 3.2e27 J/m^3`, which corresponds to `a ~= 1.9e-14 m` if one naively applies the plate formula far outside ordinary engineering regimes.
- **Assumptions:** Semiclassical QFT in locally flat regions; idealized parallel-plate Casimir formula used only for scale; ordinary material boundaries; no new field species or modified gravity; full apparatus energy must eventually be included.
- **Possible cracks:** Exotic quantum states with geometry-specific negative energy, squeezed light stress-energy, dynamic Casimir setups, quantum-information-derived energy conditions, or modified semiclassical gravity. Any crack must state sampled duration, spatial support, compensating positive energy, and complete device stress-energy.
- **Sources:** Ford & Roman 1995/1996; Pfenning & Ford 1997; Fewster 2012; Jaffe 2005; standard Casimir parallel-plate formula.

### B-010: Ordinary Positive Mass Is Real Gravity But Not Practical Localized Field Generation

- **Claim:** Ordinary positive mass-energy is the established source of gravity, but useful human-scale localized artificial gravity requires impractical mass, density, and field-gradient conditions.
- **Why it matters:** This is the sanity baseline for all localized field-generation work. If a proposed mechanism cannot beat or reshape the ordinary `M = ar^2/G` scale, it is not an engineering path.
- **Strength:** established for weak-field gravity and ordinary mass sources; engineering rejection is robust by order-of-magnitude.
- **Useful scale:** For a compact source at distance `r`, `M = ar^2/G`. Required masses are:

| Target | `0.1 m` | `1 m` | `10 m` |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `0.01g` | `1.5e7 kg` | `1.5e9 kg` | `1.5e11 kg` |
| `0.1g` | `1.5e8 kg` | `1.5e10 kg` | `1.5e12 kg` |
| `1g` | `1.5e9 kg` | `1.5e11 kg` | `1.5e13 kg` |

- **Density note:** If the field point is at the surface of a uniform sphere, `1g` at `1 m` implies average density `~3.5e10 kg/m^3`; `0.01g` at `1 m` still implies `~3.5e8 kg/m^3`. A sphere at osmium-like density (`~2.26e4 kg/m^3`) would need radius `~1.55e6 m` to provide `1g` at its surface.
- **Tidal-gradient note:** Outside a spherical/point source, the radial gradient scale is `2a/r`. Thus `1g` at `1 m` varies by about `2g` per meter; moving the mass farther away smooths the field but raises the required mass as `r^2`.
- **Assumptions:** Standard weak-field gravity; compact positive-mass source; no negative mass, active cancellation, or non-GR coupling; no engineered support structure included.
- **Possible cracks:** Shaped source geometries might improve field uniformity over a small volume, but they cannot remove the underlying mass-energy requirement. Dynamic or active sources would need separate energy, stress, radiation, and stability accounting.
- **Sources:** Carroll GR lecture notes; NIST/CODATA constants; Ozel and Freire 2016 for compact-object density context.

### B-011: Shaped Positive-Mass Fields Trade Tide For Mass And Size

- **Claim:** Rings, disks, plates, and multipole-like positive-mass layouts can reduce selected local tidal gradients, but they do not provide a practical smooth localized artificial-gravity field under known physics.
- **Why it matters:** This closes the obvious follow-up to B-010: perhaps compact mass fails, but a clever geometry might produce a flatter cabin field. The answer is that geometry helps field quality locally while worsening mass, source size, or higher-order variation.
- **Strength:** established for Newtonian/weak-field gravity; engineering rejection is robust at order-of-magnitude level.
- **Useful scale:** For a `1g` target at a point `1 m` from the source reference, a point/spherical source needs `1.47e11 kg` and has axial gradient `2g/m`. A ring with `R = sqrt(2) m` can null the axial first derivative at `z = 1 m`, but needs `7.63e11 kg` and still varies from `0.77g` at `z = 0.5 m` to `0.89g` at `z = 1.5 m`. A finite disk of radius `10 m` at `z = 1 m` needs `8.16e12 kg` and has about `+/-5.5%` axial variation over `+/-0.5 m`; a `20 m` disk needs `3.09e13 kg` for about `+/-2.6%` variation. The ideal infinite-sheet limit needs `Sigma = g/(2 pi G) ~= 2.34e10 kg/m^2`.
- **Assumptions:** Static positive mass; weak-field Newtonian gravity; no negative mass, active acceleration field, non-GR coupling, or material strength accounting; axis-field formulas used for first-pass comparisons.
- **Possible cracks:** Constrained optimization may find a better positive-mass distribution for a specified keep-out geometry, but any credible crack must report total mass, closest approach, full 3D acceleration variation, support stresses, and failure hazards.
- **Sources:** Carroll GR lecture notes; Gauss-law gravity/Bouguer plate result; standard ring and disk axis-field integrals; tidal tensor/Laplace-equation constraints.

### B-012: Positive-Mass Cabin Optimization Still Requires Asteroid-Scale Mass

- **Claim:** Constrained positive-mass source layouts outside a `1-2 m` cabin keep-out volume cannot produce useful smooth artificial gravity at practical mass scale under known physics.
- **Why it matters:** This closes the main "maybe a clever static mass layout works" blank space left by B-010 and B-011. The failure is not just a bad choice of point mass, ring, or disk; it follows from the need to create a central acceleration while suppressing tidal and lateral field variation in a source-free cabin.
- **Strength:** established as a first-pass Newtonian/weak-field engineering boundary; not a mathematical proof over every possible positive density distribution.
- **Useful scale:** For a `2 m` cabin and about `+/-10%` field variation, a distant point-source estimate needs `d ~= 20 m` and `M ~= 5.9e13 kg` for `1g`. A friendlier finite disk in a plane `2 m` behind the cabin center needs `R ~= 11.5 m`, `M ~= 1.2e13 kg` for `1g`, or `~1.2e11 kg` for `0.01g`. Tightening the finite disk to about `+/-5%` raises the `1g` mass to `~3.8e13 kg`. A numerical point-cell check over cube samples found `~+10.3%/-9.8%` field-magnitude variation for the `+/-10%` disk case, with lateral components up to `~0.073g`.
- **Assumptions:** Static ordinary positive mass; weak-field Newtonian gravity; source outside the cabin keep-out region; no negative mass, active acceleration field, non-GR coupling, or material/support accounting; finite-disk family used as a practical near-sheet comparison rather than a global optimum proof.
- **Possible cracks:** A rigorous linear-programming or optimal-control proof could tighten the lower bound, but it is unlikely to rescue positive mass. Real cracks must change the source term, effective coupling, sign, or stress-energy density rather than reshuffle ordinary mass.
- **Sources:** Carroll GR lecture notes; Gauss-law/Bouguer plate scaling; ring/disk field formulas; Yurtsever, Marzban, and Meila 2010 on gravitational inverse-problem non-uniqueness; internal E-013 disk and point-mass calculations.

### B-013: High-Energy-Density Radiation And Plasma Do Not Beat Stress-Energy Scaling

- **Claim:** Radiation cavities, ultra-intense lasers, ICF-scale plasmas, pair plasma, and antimatter/radiation storage are real stress-energy sources, but they do not provide useful localized artificial gravity under known physics.
- **Why it matters:** This closes the next tempting localized-field path after ordinary EM fields and positive mass fail: perhaps HED plasma or fusion conditions provide enough energy density, pressure, or radiation stress to matter gravitationally.
- **Strength:** established as an order-of-magnitude GR/source-term boundary; detailed plasma and radiation stress tensors can change geometry and factors of order unity, not the conclusion.
- **Useful scale:** The conventional HED scale `u ~ 1e11 J/m^3` gives `rho = u/c^2 ~= 1.1e-6 kg/m^3`, about `16.5` orders below the `1 m` spherical `1g` average `u ~= 3.2e27 J/m^3`. A traveling laser pulse at `I = 1e23 W/cm^2` has `u = I/c ~= 3.3e18 J/m^3`, still about `9` orders too low even before accounting for its microscopic volume and femtosecond duration. A `3 MJ` ICF yield has mass equivalent `~3.3e-11 kg`, so at `1 m` it sources only `~2.2e-21 m/s^2`.
- **Pressure/stress note:** Radiation pressure and plasma stresses gravitate in GR. For isotropic radiation, `p = u/3`, so pressure terms can change active-source estimates by order-unity factors. They do not supply the many-order multiplier needed for a cabin field.
- **Assumptions:** Standard GR coupling; no modified gravity; no negative-energy reservoir; no new field species; classical radiation/plasma stress-energy; weak-field order-of-magnitude estimates; pulsed sources treated by their actual integrated energy and duration.
- **Possible cracks:** Nonclassical radiation states, squeezed light, dynamic Casimir effects, engineered stress anisotropy, or precision tests of stress as a gravity source. These are possible measurement or theory cracks, not current human-scale artificial-gravity routes.
- **Sources:** National Academies 2023 HED science report; Wurzel & Hsu 2022 Lawson review; DOE NIF ignition reporting; Sadler et al. 2022 magnetized ICF modeling; Ursescu 2021 ELI-NP overview; Ehlers et al. 2005 pressure-source discussion; internal E-014 calculation.

### B-014: Nonclassical Radiation Is A Precision-Test Candidate, Not A Gravity Source

- **Claim:** Squeezed light, dynamic Casimir radiation, and related nonclassical electromagnetic states can exhibit negative or subvacuum stress-energy features, but they do not provide practical localized artificial gravity under known physics.
- **Why it matters:** This closes the main crack left by B-013. The issue is not whether nonclassical stress-energy exists; it does. The issue is whether it supplies large, controllable, separable, long-duration source terms. Current theory and experiments say no.
- **Strength:** established for the source-scale rejection; still open as a precision semiclassical-gravity or quantum-inequality testbed.
- **Useful scale:** In the one-mode Ford/Roman model, `rho = (hbar omega / V) [n + R cos(...)]` and `rho_min = -(hbar omega / V)(R - n)`. For a single-mode squeezed vacuum, `n = sinh^2 r` and `R - n = sinh r cosh r - sinh^2 r`, which approaches `1/2` at high squeezing. A `15 dB` squeezed vacuum has `r ~= 1.73`, `n ~= 7.4`, and `R - n ~= 0.48`. With an optimistic `1064 nm` cubic-wavelength mode volume, `0.5 h nu / lambda^3 ~= 0.08 J/m^3`, roughly `4e28` below the `1 m` spherical `1g` benchmark `u ~= 3.2e27 J/m^3`. The same toy estimate reaches the benchmark only at `lambda ~= 7.5e-14 m`, before accounting for production, localization, compensating positive energy, apparatus energy, and quantum-inequality constraints.
- **Dynamic-Casimir note:** Superconducting-circuit DCE experiments create real microwave photons and two-mode squeezing through a modulated boundary. That is strong evidence for nonclassical field dynamics, but an `11 GHz` photon has only `~7.3e-24 J`; the generated radiation is far too small as a gravitational source.
- **Assumptions:** Standard semiclassical stress-energy coupling; no modified gravity; no new field species; normal-ordered negative energy treated relative to the vacuum; complete source includes pumps, boundaries, detectors, and compensating positive energy; no FTL claim.
- **Possible cracks:** A clean phase-tagged precision experiment might test semiclassical source accounting, pressure/stress gravity, detector response to subvacuum states, or QI sampling assumptions. That would update H-003/H-005, not produce artificial gravity unless the measured effect violates existing scale expectations by enormous factors.
- **Sources:** Ford and Roman 2007; Wilson et al. 2011; Ford and Roman 2013; Fewster and Roman 2003; Schnabel 2017; Maclay and Davis 2019 as a provocative source-quality check; internal E-015 calculation.

### B-015: Modulated Nonclassical EM Direct Gravity Signals Are Below Detector Reach

- **Claim:** A phase-tagged squeezed-light, dynamic-Casimir, or related nonclassical EM source is not directly detectable as a gravitational source with near-term sensors under standard coupling.
- **Why it matters:** This narrows the remaining "precision-source experiment" crack from B-014. The problem is not only that nonclassical source terms are small; even deliberately generous ordinary energy modulation is far below current gravity detector thresholds, while mundane electromagnetic, thermal, acoustic, and mechanical systematics are much larger.
- **Strength:** established as an order-of-magnitude detector/source-energy boundary; open only as a future bookkeeping/null-protocol or beyond-standard-coupling test.
- **Useful scale:** For an optimistic compact modulated source energy `deltaE`, `a ~= G deltaE/(c^2 r^2)`. Representative accelerations are `7.4e-22 m/s^2` for `1 J` at `1 mm`, `7.4e-19 m/s^2` for `1 kJ` at `1 mm`, and `7.4e-16 m/s^2` for `1 MJ` at `1 mm`. Matching the Panda et al. atom-interferometer benchmark `6.2 nm/s^2` requires `~8.3e12 J` at `1 mm`, `~8.3e16 J` at `0.1 m`, or `~8.3e18 J` at `1 m`. For a `~300 nm` silica nanosphere, a `1 J`, `1 mm` source-energy modulation produces force near `1.8e-37 N`, far below zeptonewton-scale force sensing.
- **Semiclassical note:** Kuo and Ford show that states with negative local energy density can have large stress-tensor fluctuations, so a smooth classical metric sourced only by `<T_ab>` may not be the correct observable. This makes source bookkeeping more important, not easier.
- **Assumptions:** Standard GR/semiclassical coupling; no anomalously enhanced EM-gravity coupling; compact-source estimate used as an optimistic bound; detector benchmarks treated at the acceleration/force scale before full systematic noise; complete apparatus energy, pump power, boundaries, heat, and recoil must be included for any real design.
- **Possible cracks:** A future detector many orders more sensitive, a source with enormous modulated stored energy, or a replicated nonstandard coupling. More realistically, the near-term crack is a rigorous null-protocol: compare identical mean-energy coherent and nonclassical states, blind phase reversals, dummy heat loads, shielding variation, distance scaling, and stress-tensor variance predictions.
- **Sources:** Ratzel, Wilkens, and Menzel 2016; Kuo and Ford 1993; Panda et al. 2024; Ranjit et al. 2016; Lee et al. 2020; internal E-016 calculation.

### B-016: Nonclassical State Labels Do Not Replace A Closed Stress-Energy Ledger

- **Claim:** A gravity or semiclassical-source claim based on squeezed light or dynamic-Casimir radiation is uninterpretable unless the full apparatus stress-energy history is closed; switching nonclassicality alone does not isolate a new deterministic gravitational source.
- **Why it matters:** This closes the bookkeeping gap left by B-014 and B-015. Standard semiclassical gravity responds at leading order to `<T_ab>`, not squeezing in isolation. Two states matched in mean stress-energy have the same leading mean metric prediction; differences in variance belong to a noise-kernel/correlation calculation, not an added classical force.
- **Strength:** established for conservation and mean-field bookkeeping; the observable consequences of stress-tensor fluctuations remain theory-dependent and unclear for a real laboratory source.
- **Useful scale:** A `13 dB`, `1550 nm` single-mode squeezed vacuum has `r ~= 1.50`, mean occupation `n ~= 4.5`, mean excitation energy `~5.8e-19 J` per mode, and a most-negative normal-ordered trough no larger than about `6.4e-20 J` per mode in the one-mode model. Even an illustrative `100 MHz` stream is only `~5.8e-11 W` of mean excitation, versus the demonstrated `12 mW` external pump: a ratio `~4.8e-9`. At `1 kHz` switching, the pump delivers `12 uJ` each cycle, about `2e13` times one mode's mean squeezed excitation. These are comparison bounds, not a detailed cavity energy reconstruction.
- **Required ledger:** field `<T_ab>` by spatial mode and bandwidth; normal-ordered/subvacuum term and sampling; connected stress correlation/noise kernel; pump and local-oscillator energy; cavity/mirror/crystal or SQUID/flux-line energy; emitted radiation and absorption; heat and thermal expansion; mechanical recoil/strain; shielding currents and cables; source/absorber history; uncertainties and phase transfer functions.
- **Control requirement:** At minimum compare source-off, pump-on/no-squeezing detuned, coherent/thermal matched-mean-energy, phase-reversed squeezing, dummy heat, dummy electromagnetic drive, and distance/orientation changes. Each state must report both total apparatus-energy modulation and field-state modulation.
- **Possible cracks:** An equal-mean coherent/squeezed comparison could probe a stress-tensor noise or source-probe correlation if a specific smeared noise-kernel observable is derived. This is unclear, not an artificial-gravity mechanism, and does not evade B-008, B-014, or B-015.
- **Sources:** Schönbeck, Thies, and Schnabel 2018; Wilson et al. 2011; Johansson et al. 2010; Hu and Verdaguer 2008; Kuo and Ford 1993; internal E-017 ledger.

### B-017: A Distinct Nonclassical Noise Kernel Is Not An Isolated Gravity Signal

- **Claim:** Equal-mean coherent and squeezed states can have different connected stress-energy correlations, but this does not yield a practically isolatable gravitational probe spectrum under standard coupling.
- **Why it matters:** This resolves the formal blank space left by B-016. A different noise kernel exists in a transparent one-mode model, yet the same field fluctuations drive ordinary radiation pressure, and the gravitational transfer is suppressed by `G/c^2` to an unusable scale.
- **Strength:** established for the idealized cycle-averaged single-mode screen and order-of-magnitude back-action comparison; uncertain for the exact exterior spectrum of a complete conserved cavity-plus-wall system.
- **Useful scale:** With matched mean occupation `N`, `Var_coh(n)=N` and `Var_sq(n)=2N(N+1)`. Assuming a cavity correlation time `1/kappa`, `S_E(Omega)=2(hbar omega)^2 Var(n) kappa/(kappa^2+Omega^2)`. At `1550 nm`, `N=4.5`, `kappa/2pi=100 MHz`, and `r=1 mm`, the optimistic standard-gravity acceleration amplitudes at low frequency are only `~1.14e-44` and `~3.78e-44 m/s^2/sqrt(Hz)`, respectively.
- **Back-action boundary:** A reflecting in-field probe has `delta F_g/delta F_EM ~ GmL/(2c^2r^2)`, about `3.7e-24` for `m=1 kg`, `L=1 cm`, and `r=1 mm`. Direct optomechanical back-action therefore carries the state tag at least `~23` orders more strongly in this generous comparison. Shielding the probe transfers the problem to cavity-wall recoil, supports, leakage, and conservation accounting.
- **Assumptions:** One stationary mode; identical spatial mode and mean occupation; optical-cycle averaging removes `2omega` anomalous terms; exponential number correlation with decay rate `kappa`; compact, quasi-static Newtonian gravitational propagation; no anomalous coupling; no claim that a field-only energy fluctuation is the full conserved exterior source.
- **Possible cracks:** A complete conserved-system calculation could reveal a residual exterior quadrupole or retarded correlation after monopole cancellation or redistribution. That is a precision quantum-gravity question, not an artificial-gravity mechanism, and it cannot evade the absolute source scale.
- **Sources:** Hu and Verdaguer 2003/2008; Clark et al. 2016; Yap et al. 2019; internal E-018 calculation.

### B-018: Conservation Preserves A Formal Tidal Noise Channel, Not A Detectable Signal

- **Claim:** A compact divergence-free electromagnetic/apparatus stress-energy perturbation can retain a nonzero exterior tidal response after monopole and dipole cancellation, but projecting realistic coherent or squeezed photon-number noise through that response remains fantastically below useful sensitivity.
- **Why it matters:** This resolves the main formal gap left by B-017. The field-only compact-energy estimate is not a consistent source: it violates harmonic gauge because it changes energy without the required flux and stress. Completing the tensor restores consistency and leaves a higher-moment tidal observable, not a changing gravitational monopole.
- **Strength:** strongly modeled for an effective one-dimensional total-apparatus source in linearized GR; not a microscopic three-dimensional mirror/pump/absorber calculation.
- **Useful scale:** For `deltaE=1 J`, `L=1 cm`, `f=100 MHz`, and an axial probe at `1 m`, the conserved model gives `|K_xx| ~= 2.04e-31 s^-2`; the source conservation residual is below `7e-10`, and the retarded harmonic-gauge residual converges from `~1e-5` at 501 cells to `~3e-9` at 4001 cells while the tidal result is stable to better than `1e-5`. Projecting the E-018 `1550 nm`, `N=4.5`, `kappa/2pi=100 MHz` model gives one-sided relative-acceleration ASD `~3.13e-54 m/s^2/sqrt(Hz)` for coherent light and `~1.04e-53 m/s^2/sqrt(Hz)` for squeezed vacuum over a `1 m` baseline. A close exterior probe at `x=6 mm` with a `0.1 mm` baseline reaches only `~2.57e-46 m/s^2/sqrt(Hz)` for squeezed vacuum.
- **Stress boundary:** Dynamic enhancement is paid for by longitudinal apparatus stress. In the chosen source family, `integral|Txx|/deltaE` is `~2.34e-9` at `1 MHz`, `~2.34e-5` at `100 MHz`, `~0.234` at `10 GHz`, and grows as `(omega L/c)^2`. Arbitrarily increasing frequency without accounting for support strength and baseline apparatus energy is not a physical amplification mechanism.
- **Assumptions:** Standard linearized GR; harmonic convention and retarded Green function; effective compact line source with `T00`, `T0x`, and `Txx`; signed quantities are perturbations around a positive apparatus baseline; one-dimensional longitudinal tidal observable; exponential photon-number correlation inherited from E-018; no anomalous coupling.
- **Possible cracks:** A microscopic finite-radius cavity with explicit mirrors, springs, pump, loss port, and absorber could redistribute the residual tidal multipoles or alter angular structure. It would need to change the conserved transfer by many orders of magnitude to affect detectability, and cannot restore a changing monopole in a closed system.
- **Sources:** Carroll 1997; Ratzel, Wilkens, and Menzel 2016; Phillips and Hu 2000; Hu and Verdaguer 2003/2008; Gratus, Pinto, and Talaganis 2020; internal E-019 tensor calculation.

### B-019: Canonical Chameleon Screening Trades Coupling Strength For Body Universality

- **Claim:** In a passive matter-bounded cabin, the canonical inverse-power chameleon cannot provide useful, approximately universal body-scale acceleration. Increasing microscopic coupling screens macroscopic occupants and sources, so their effective scalar charges fall and the acceleration saturates at an object-dependent value.
- **Why it matters:** This is the first explicit E-009 modified-coupling screen. A new force can evade ordinary `G/c^2` source-energy scaling, but screening introduced to satisfy fifth-force tests can remove exactly the universal macroscopic response required for artificial gravity.
